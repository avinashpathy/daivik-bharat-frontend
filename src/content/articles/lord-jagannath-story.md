King **Indradyumna** of Avanti was a great devotee of Lord Vishnu, and he longed to see the Lord with his own eyes. One day he was told that Vishnu was worshipped in his truest form as **Neela Madhava** somewhere in Odisha. The king at once sent his head priest, **Vidyapati**, to find him.

After a long and tiring journey, Vidyapati reached Odisha. There he met the tribal chieftain **Vishvavasu**, who was secretly guarding Neela Madhava. Vidyapati begged to be shown the place of worship, but Vishvavasu refused. Vidyapati stayed on in the village, fell in love with Vishvavasu's daughter Lalitha, and in time married her. Soon after the wedding, he asked his wife to persuade her father to grant him a single glimpse of Neela Madhava.

## The hidden cave

At his daughter's insistence, Vishvavasu agreed, on one condition: Vidyapati would be taken there blindfolded. Vidyapati carried mustard seeds and dropped them along the way so that he could find the route again. When the blindfold came off inside the secret cave, he was spellbound by the magnificent image of Neela Madhava.

Vidyapati sent word to the king, who came at once. But to Indradyumna's disappointment, the deity had disappeared. The king resolved to fast unto death on Mount Neela. Then he heard a voice promising that he would see the deity. He performed a horse sacrifice and built a temple, and Narada installed an idol of Sri Narasimha in it. Still, time passed without any sign of Neela Madhava, and the frustrated king lay down on a bed of kusha grass to fast until death.

## Daru-brahman

**Lord Jagannath** appeared to the king in a dream and told him that he would come floating in from the sea, in a wooden form called **Daru-brahman**, at a place called Bankimuhan. The king went there and found a huge log bearing the marks of a conch, a club, a discus and a lotus. Many men and elephants tried to move it, but it would not budge.

That night the Lord spoke to Indradyumna in a dream again. He told him to bring Vishvavasu, who had served Neela Madhava, and to place a golden chariot before the log. The king did so, and Daru-brahman was lifted onto the chariot with ease.

## The unfinished forms

Indradyumna summoned the finest sculptors to carve the deity from Daru-brahman, but their chisels shattered as soon as they began. Then the Lord himself arrived in disguise as an old artisan who called himself **Ananta Maharana**. (According to the Narada Purana, this was **Vishwakarma**, the craftsman of the gods.) He said he could carve the deities if he was left to work behind closed doors for 21 days.

The old sculptor took Daru-brahman into the temple and the doors were shut. After 14 days the king could no longer hear the sound of any tools, and he grew anxious. He had the doors forced open.

Vishwakarma was gone. In his place stood three forms: **Lord Jagannath, Maa Subhadra and Lord Balabhadra**, their fingers and toes unfinished. Believing he had committed a grave offence, the king once again lay down on kusha grass to fast unto death. Lord Jagannath appeared to him in a dream and told him that he is eternally present here in Nilachala as Daru-brahman, in the form of Jagannath. "I have no material hands and feet," the Lord said, "but with my transcendental senses I accept everything my devotees offer me."

It is believed that the wooden deities worshipped today in the Jagannath Temple at Puri carry on this same form.
