In the Shakta tradition of Hinduism, the Shakti Peethas are pilgrimage sites devoted to Goddess Sati, an aspect of Adi Shakti. Sati is remembered as fiery and fearsome in her anger, yet loving and deeply benevolent. Lists differ between texts, but a common count is 51 Shakti Peethas:

- **Adi Shakti Peethas:** 4
- **Maha Shakti Peethas** (the Ashtadasha, or eighteen, Shakti Peethas): 18
- **Shakti Peethas:** 29

## The story, as the Puranas tell it

Sati Devi, daughter of Prajapati Daksha (a son of Brahma), was an incarnation of Adi Shakti. After years of prayer and meditation, she married Lord Shiva.

At a yajña held by Agni, everyone rose when Daksha arrived, except Lord Shiva. Daksha took this as an insult. When Daksha later organised a yajña of his own, he invited neither Shiva nor Sati. Shiva refused to go, but Sati went anyway. Her family did not receive her properly, and her father insulted Shiva in front of everyone. Unable to bear the insult to her husband, and overcome by anger, shame and regret, Sati gave up her life in the sacrificial fire.

When Shiva heard what had happened, he was consumed by rage. From his jata (matted hair) he brought forth Veerabhadra, who killed Daksha. Shiva then lifted Sati's body from the fire and began the Tandava, a dance that terrified the worlds. To stop it, Lord Vishnu sent his Sudarshana Chakra, which cut Sati's body into pieces. According to the tradition, the places where those pieces fell, across today's India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Nepal and Tibet, are the Shakti Peethas.

## Adi Shakti Peethas

- Bimala Shakti Peetha, inside the Puri Jagannath Temple, Odisha
- Tara Tarini Sthana Peetha, Odisha
- Kamakhya Temple, Guwahati, Assam
- Kalighat Kali Temple, West Bengal

- Sri Kanchi Kamakshi Amman Temple, Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu
- Sri Srunkhala Devi Temple, Pandua, West Bengal
- Sri Chamundeshwari Devi Temple, Chamundi Hill, Mysuru, Karnataka
- Shri Jogulamba Temple, Alampur, Telangana
- Sri Bhramaramba Devi Temple, Srisailam, Andhra Pradesh
- Shree Mahalaxmi Temple, Kolhapur, Maharashtra
- Mahurye Ekaveerika Temple / Renuka Devi Temple, Mahur, Maharashtra
- Shri Gadhkalika Mata Temple / Mahakali Devi, Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh
- Puruhutika Devi Temple, Pithapuram, Andhra Pradesh
- Biraja Devi / Biraja Kshetra, Jajpur, Odisha
- Manikyamba Devi Temple, Draksharamam, Andhra Pradesh
- Kamarupa / Kamakhya Temple, Guwahati, Assam
- Madhaveswari Devi Temple, Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh
- Jwalamukhi / Vaishnavi Devi, Kangra, Himachal Pradesh
- Mangla Gauri Temple, Gaya, Bihar
- Vishalakshi Temple, Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh
- Sharada Peeth (Saraswati Devi), Kashmir
- Shri Shankari Devi Temple, Trincomalee, Sri Lanka
