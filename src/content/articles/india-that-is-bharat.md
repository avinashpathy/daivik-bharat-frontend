Most of us grew up using "India" and "Bharat" as if they were the same word. They are both official, but they have very different histories. Here's what we found when we went looking.

## What the Constitution says

The very first article of the Constitution names the country twice:

> India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.
> — Article 1(1), Constitution of India

So both names are official, and neither is a translation of the other.

## Where "Bharat" comes from

The name is ancient, and more than one tradition explains it.

- **The Puranas describe a land called Bhāratavarṣa.** The Vishnu Purana (2.3.1) defines it as the country north of the ocean and south of the snowy mountains, where the descendants of Bharata live:

  > उत्तरं यत्समुद्रस्य हिमाद्रेश्चैव दक्षिणम् ।
  > वर्षं तद्भारतं नाम भारती यत्र सन्ततिः ॥

- **Which Bharata?** The Mahabharata traces the line to Bharata, son of King Dushyanta and Shakuntala. The Bhagavata Purana and Jain tradition name a different Bharata, the eldest son of Rishabhadeva. We found both explanations in circulation, and the texts themselves don't settle it as one.
- **Even older, the Bharatas were a people.** The Rigveda mentions the Bharatas as a tribe. Their king Sudas wins the Battle of the Ten Kings in Mandala 7, hymn 18.

## Where "India" comes from

"India" comes from the river Sindhu (the Indus). Persian speakers pronounced the "s" as an "h", which gave "Hindu". The Greeks took the word up as "Indos", and from there it reached Latin and English as "India". So "India" began as an outsider's name for the people beyond the Indus.

## The 1949 debate

When the Constituent Assembly took up Article 1 on 18 September 1949, members argued over which name should come first. H. V. Kamath proposed an amendment to read "Bharat, or in the English language, India". Others, including Seth Govind Das, argued for putting Bharat first. The amendment was defeated, and the wording we have today was adopted.

## Where we've landed, for now

- **What the sources say:** both names are official. "Bharat" comes from our own textual tradition, and "India" comes from the Indus, through Persian and Greek.
- **What we're still unsure about:** why the Assembly settled on this exact wording, and how the members who voted it through saw the two names. We want to read the full debate before we say more.
